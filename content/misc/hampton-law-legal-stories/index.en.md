## Legal Scenarios & Strategies

### 1. "No Drop" Policies on Domestic Violence Cases (2021)

**Scenario:** Police arrest an individual for family violence after a verbal dispute, and prosecutors pursue charges despite the complainant recanting.

**Legal Strategy:**
- **"No Drop" Policies**: DA offices (e.g., Tarrant County) enforce zero-tolerance policies, prosecuting domestic violence allegations regardless of complainant wishes.
- **Affidavit of Non-Prosecution (ANP)**: A complainant's ANP does not bind the prosecution; charging authority rests solely with the State.
- **Defense Strategy**: Challenge the evidence under Tex. Penal Code § 22.01, showing the absence of bodily injury or intentional physical contact to hold the State to its burden of proof beyond a reasonable doubt.

### 2. 3 Strategies To Beat A DWI Case in Texas (2026)

**Scenario:** A motorist stopped for DWI faces chemical testing and driver's license suspension proceedings.

**Legal Strategy:**
- **Statutory Thresholds**: DWI is a Class B misdemeanor under Tex. Penal Code § 49.04, enhanced to Class A if blood alcohol concentration (BAC) is 0.15 or greater.
- **Core Defenses**:
  1. *Absence of Chemical Testing*: Without breath or blood specimens, the state relies entirely on subjective officer impressions.
  2. *Vehicle Operation*: The state must prove actual operation in a public place; occupying a parked car with climate control running does not establish operation as a matter of law.
  3. *Retrograde Extrapolation*: Tests hours after a stop reflect BAC at testing rather than driving, requiring vulnerable retrograde extrapolation.

### 3. 4 Ways To Defeat A Juvenile Assault Charge In Texas (2025)

**Scenario:** A student faces juvenile assault charges under a campus zero-tolerance policy after intervening in a fight to halt bullying.

**Legal Strategy:**
- **Classification Range**: Under Tex. Penal Code § 22.01 and Tex. Fam. Code Ch. 54, juvenile assault spans from Class C misdemeanor (offensive contact) to third-degree felony (assault on a public servant or educator).
- **Core Defenses**:
  1. *Lack of Intent*: Contact was accidental or defensive, not intentional or knowing.
  2. *Absence of Bodily Injury*: Disproving physical pain or impairment defeats Class A assault allegations.
  3. *Defense of Others / Necessity*: Using reasonable force to prevent imminent bodily injury is justified under Tex. Penal Code §§ 9.22 and 9.33.
  4. *Self-Defense*: Responding to an initial aggressor negates culpability under Tex. Penal Code § 9.31.

### 4. 5 Legal Myths Cops Hope You Still Believe

**Scenario:** During a stop or arrest, officers claim warrantless authority to search an individual's bags, search a phone, or enter a home.

**Legal Strategy:**
- **Search Incident to Arrest**: Officers may search an arrestee's person and grab area (*Chimel v. California*), but closed luggage under exclusive police control requires a warrant or valid inventory policy (*United States v. Chadwick*).
- **Miranda Limits**: Miranda applies strictly to custodial interrogation, not routine investigatory detentions (*Terry v. Ohio*) or traffic stops (*Berkemer v. McCarty*).
- **Digital Privacy**: *Riley v. California*, 573 U.S. 373 (2014), requires a warrant to search a phone; passcodes receive stronger Fifth Amendment protection than compelled biometrics.
- **Terry Frisk**: Requires specific, articulable reasonable suspicion that the individual is armed and dangerous.
- **Residential Entry**: An arrest warrant authorizes entering the suspect's residence only (*Payton v. New York*); entering a third party's home requires a search warrant (*Steagald v. United States*).

### 5. 5 Life-Saving Tips If You Get Arrested (2023)

**Scenario:** An individual is arrested, placed in a patrol car, and questioned by police.

**Legal Strategy:**
- **Invocation of Rights**: Unambiguously invoke Fifth Amendment silence and Sixth Amendment counsel under *Edwards v. Arizona* ("I am exercising my right to remain silent and request an attorney before answering questions").
- **Preservation of Demeanor**: Composure on body and dash cameras neutralizes jury prejudice and contrasts with officer overreach.
- **Recorded Jail Communications**: Detainee calls, messages, and visitation booths are recorded and monitored by prosecutors; admissions will be introduced at trial.
- **Interrogation Caution**: Decline informal interviews with jailhouse detectives absent counsel to prevent coerced or contextualized admissions.

### 6. 5 Steps You MUST Take If You're Under Criminal Investigation (2026)

**Scenario:** A detective asks the target of an ongoing investigation for an informal interview to "hear their side of the story."

**Legal Strategy:**
- **Interrogation Traps**: Informal interviews elicit un-Mirandized statements that lock targets into admissions or impeachment traps.
- **Third-Party Disclosures**: Statements to friends, family, or coworkers are non-privileged and admissible as party-opponent admissions under Tex. R. Evid. 801(e)(2).
- **Legal Intermediation**: Counsel can discover the probe's scope and proffer facts without exposing the target to self-incrimination.
- **Pre-Indictment Intervention**: Early representation permits Grand Jury packet submissions seeking a "No Bill" before formal indictment.

### 7. 5 Steps Your Criminal Attorney Should Do Now! (2022)

**Scenario:** A defendant facing criminal charges requires an aggressive pretrial defense plan to evaluate the prosecution's evidence.

**Legal Strategy:**
- **Discovery Demands**: Compel state disclosures under Tex. Code Crim. Proc. art. 39.14 (Michael Morton Act), including police reports, body camera footage, dispatch audio, and exculpatory *Brady* material.
- **Evidentiary Triangulation**: Cross-reference physical evidence and footage against charging instruments (Information or Indictment) and witness statements to isolate contradictions.
- **Constitutional Suppression**: File pretrial motions to suppress under Fourth and Fifth Amendments and Tex. Code Crim. Proc. art. 38.23 for unlawful stops, warrantless searches, or involuntary statements.
- **Pretrial Disposition**: Pursue pretrial diversion, specialty courts, or Grand Jury "No Bill" presentations prior to setting trial.

### 8. 5 Tips for How to Talk to Cops Like a Lawyer!

**Scenario:** An officer approaches an individual in public or at home to initiate informal questioning.

**Legal Strategy:**
- **Consensual Encounter vs. Detention**: In a consensual encounter, an individual may decline interaction and walk away (*Florida v. Royer*); clarify status by asking, "Am I free to leave?"
- **Unambiguous Invocation**: If detained under *Terry v. Ohio*, assert Fifth Amendment silence and request counsel before answering questions.
- **Self-Defense Protocol**: In force incidents, call 911 to report the threat and need for medical/police aid, then withhold narrative statements until counsel is present.
- **Knock-and-Talk Limits**: Officers at a doorway lack warrantless entry authority absent consent or exigency (*Florida v. Jardines*); residents may decline to open the door.

### 9. 5 Ways To Beat A Drug Possession Charge! (2021)

**Scenario:** Police discover controlled substances in a car with multiple occupants, leading to felony possession charges against a passenger.

**Legal Strategy:**
- **Possession Standard**: Under Tex. Penal Code § 1.07(a)(39) and Tex. Health & Safety Code § 481.002(38), possession requires intentional or knowing care, custody, control, or management; mere proximity in a shared vehicle does not prove affirmative links.
- **Suppression Remedies**: Challenge vehicle stops lacking reasonable suspicion or searches exceeding consent or automobile exception limits under Tex. Code Crim. Proc. art. 38.23.
- **Statutory Defenses**: Assert entrapment under Tex. Penal Code § 8.06 where police induced the offense, or invoke medical use under the Texas Compassionate Use Act (Tex. Health & Safety Code Ch. 487).
- **Pretrial Diversion**: Complete first-offender diversion to secure dismissal and statutory expunction.

### 10. AI License Plate Readers & False Arrests

**Scenario:** A motorist is stopped at gunpoint in a felony traffic stop after an automated license plate reader (ALPR) misidentifies their vehicle as stolen.

**Legal Strategy:**
- **Reasonable Suspicion Flaws**: ALPR character-recognition errors or stale database entries fail to support reasonable suspicion if officers do not visually verify the plate before stopping the car.
- **Search Protocol**: Refuse consent to searches; automated database errors provide no independent probable cause to search closed compartments once the plate mismatch appears.
- **Post-Incident Remedies**: Preserve dispatch audio, CAD logs, and ALPR hit data for Fourth Amendment suppression and evaluate potential 42 U.S.C. § 1983 civil rights claims against qualified immunity defenses.

### 11. Accidental Killing or Involuntary Manslaughter? (2021)

**Scenario:** An individual faces potential homicide prosecution following an unintentional death during an incident.

**Legal Strategy:**
- **Accident vs. Culpable Mens Rea**: Tex. Penal Code § 6.03 distinguishes a non-criminal accident (lawful conduct conducted with reasonable care) from Manslaughter and Criminally Negligent Homicide.
- **Recklessness Threshold**: Manslaughter under Tex. Penal Code § 19.04 is a second-degree felony (2–20 years imprisonment) requiring proof that the actor consciously disregarded a substantial, unjustifiable risk of death.
- **Grand Jury Representation**: Submit an exculpatory packet under Tex. Code Crim. Proc. art. 20A demonstrating lawful conduct and lack of conscious risk disregard to obtain a "No Bill."

### 12. Accused of Sexual Assault in Texas? Winning Defenses You MUST Know (2026)

**Scenario:** An individual is accused of sexual assault based solely on testimonial claims without corroborating forensic evidence.

**Legal Strategy:**
- **Statutory Offense**: Tex. Penal Code § 22.011 defines Sexual Assault as a second-degree felony, enhanced to first-degree under § 22.021 (Aggravated Sexual Assault) upon proof of deadly weapons, serious bodily injury, or statutory vulnerability.
- **Core Defenses**:
  1. *Consent*: Contemporaneous evidence (texts, location metadata, witnesses) establishes consent; retroactive regret does not negate consent.
  2. *Alibi & Digital Timelines*: Digital evidence (cell tower data, GPS, surveillance) conclusively disproves physical proximity at the alleged offense time.
  3. *Romeo and Juliet Defense*: Tex. Penal Code § 22.011(e) provides an affirmative defense where the actor was no more than three years older than the victim and the victim was at least 14.
- **Procedural Caution**: Decline pre-arrest police interrogations; forensic interviews and pretext phone calls are used to manufacture contradictory statements.

### 13. Adult Court vs Juvenile Court - What Is The Difference? (2026)

**Scenario:** A minor is detained for alleged delinquent conduct and held in a juvenile facility awaiting adjudication.

**Legal Strategy:**
- **Governing Law**: Juvenile matters are civil-quasi-criminal proceedings under Title 3 of the Texas Family Code (Tex. Fam. Code Ch. 51–54), emphasizing rehabilitation over punitive sentencing.
- **Detention & Bail**: Juveniles have no constitutional or statutory right to cash bond; release is evaluated at mandatory 10-day detention hearings under Tex. Fam. Code § 54.01 based on flight risk, parental supervision, and public danger.
- **Procedural Hurdles**: Facilities often tie release recommendations to internal behavioral levels or judicial policies.
- **Transfer & Certification**: Serious felony allegations carry risk of discretionary transfer to adult criminal court under Tex. Fam. Code § 54.02.

### 14. Aggravated Assault in Texas - How to Beat Your Case! (2026)

**Scenario:** A confrontation escalates into second-degree felony aggravated assault charges based on alleged serious bodily injury or weapon display.

**Legal Strategy:**
- **Statutory Elements**: Tex. Penal Code § 22.02 elevates assault to a second-degree felony (or first-degree under specific circumstances) if the actor:
  1. *Causes Serious Bodily Injury (SBI)*: Defined in § 1.07(a)(46) as injury creating substantial risk of death, permanent disfigurement, or protracted impairment of bodily function; minor cuts or transient pain do not suffice.
  2. *Uses/Exhibits a Deadly Weapon*: Defined under § 1.07(a)(17) as any firearm or instrument capable of causing death or SBI in its use.
  3. *Assault by Threat with a Deadly Weapon*: Displaying a weapon during a confrontational dispute.
- **Defense Strategy**: Scrutinize medical records to challenge SBI, establish justification under Tex. Penal Code Ch. 9, and cross-examine witness bias.

### 15. Am I blocking you from working? (First Amendment & Cops)

**Scenario:** An individual filming police uses profanity or gestures toward officers and is arrested for interference with public duties or disorderly conduct.

**Legal Strategy:**
- **First Amendment Protection**: Verbal criticism, profanity, and gestures directed at police officers are constitutionally protected speech and not fighting words (*City of Houston v. Hill*, 482 U.S. 451 (1987)).
- **Interference with Public Duties**: Tex. Penal Code § 38.15 requires actual physical interference; non-obstructive recording from a reasonable distance is explicitly protected under § 38.15(d).
- **Remedies**: An arrest based strictly on protected speech or recording violates the First and Fourth Amendments; file a motion to suppress/dismiss and evaluate 42 U.S.C. § 1983 civil rights claims.

### 16. Are Public Defenders Actually Free? (2023)

**Scenario:** An indigent defendant requests appointed counsel under the impression that public defender services carry no future financial liability.

**Legal Strategy:**
- **Statutory Fee Recoupment**: Under Tex. Code Crim. Proc. art. 26.05(g), if a court finds a defendant has financial resources to offset legal costs, it may order reimbursement of appointed attorney fees as court costs or a probation condition.
- **Revocation Limitations**: Probation cannot be revoked for nonpayment absent inquiry into intentional refusal versus indigent inability to pay (*Bearden v. Georgia*, 461 U.S. 660 (1983)); assessed fees remain enforceable civil judgments.
- **Strategic Caution**: Defendants must factor potential fee recoupment into overall defense strategy and financial planning.

### 17. Arrested And Your Property Was Taken By The Police? (2022)

**Scenario:** Following dismissal of criminal charges, law enforcement retains personal property, currency, or vehicles seized during an arrest.

**Legal Strategy:**
- **Return of Evidence**: Property is not released automatically after dismissal; counsel must file a Motion for Disposition of Seized Property under Tex. Code Crim. Proc. art. 47.01 et seq. to secure a judicial release order.
- **Civil Asset Forfeiture (Chapter 59)**: Under Tex. Code Crim. Proc. Ch. 59, the state may pursue civil forfeiture against alleged felony contraband independently of criminal case outcomes.
- **20-Day Answer Deadline**: A civil forfeiture petition requires an answer within 20 days of service; failure to timely answer results in default forfeiture despite criminal innocence.

### 18. Arrested For A Crime In Arlington, Texas? Do This Now!

**Scenario:** An individual is arrested in Arlington, booked into the Tarrant County Jail, and charged with misdemeanor or felony offenses.

**Legal Strategy:**
- **ALR Hearing Deadline**: In DWI arrests, a request for an Administrative License Revocation (ALR) hearing must be filed with DPS within 15 days of receiving the Notice of Suspension (Tex. Transp. Code § 724.041).
- **Operation Requirement**: Tex. Penal Code § 49.04 mandates proof of vehicle "operation"; stationary vehicles with idling engines require factual scrutiny under totality-of-circumstances case law.
- **Procedural Protocol**: Invoke Fifth Amendment rights immediately, decline jailhouse interviews, and seek pretrial diversion or grand jury presentation to secure expunction under Tex. Code Crim. Proc. Ch. 55A.

### 19. Arrested For A Crime? When Do You Tell Your Side Of The Story? (2022)

**Scenario:** A criminal defendant seeks to communicate their innocence directly to investigating detectives, prosecutors, or the trial jury.

**Legal Strategy:**
- **Interrogation Hazards**: Direct statements to police create impeachment material and partial admissions under Tex. Code Crim. Proc. art. 38.22.
- **Trial Testimony Risks**: Waiving the Fifth Amendment to testify exposes the defendant to cross-examination and extraneous offenses under Tex. R. Evid. 404(b) and 609.
- **Strategic Proffers**: Introduce exculpatory facts through independent witnesses, defense investigation, and counsel-led grand jury packets to shield the client from cross-examination.

### 20. Assault By Contact Or Assault by Threat? Do This To Win Your Case (2026)

**Scenario:** An individual is charged with assault following non-injurious physical contact or an alleged verbal threat.

**Legal Strategy:**
- **Statutory Categories (Tex. Penal Code § 22.01)**:
  - *Assault by Threat (§ 22.01(a)(2))*: Intentionally or knowingly threatening imminent bodily injury; Class C misdemeanor.
  - *Assault by Contact (§ 22.01(a)(3))*: Intentionally or knowingly causing physical contact reasonably regarded as offensive or provocative; Class C misdemeanor.
- **Plea Trap**: Paying a Class C fine constitutes a final conviction; if entered with an affirmative finding of family violence (Tex. Code Crim. Proc. art. 42.013), it creates a permanent domestic violence record barring expunction and firearm ownership.
- **Remedy**: Contest threat imminence or offensive contact, seek deferred disposition to preserve expunction under Tex. Code Crim. Proc. Ch. 55A, or pursue dismissal.

### 21. Assault Or Self-Defense? Learn How To Dismiss Your Charges! (2021)

**Scenario:** An individual repels an attack during an altercation, but responding officers arrest them for assault.

**Legal Strategy:**
- **Statutory Justification**: Tex. Penal Code § 9.31 justifies force when the actor reasonably believes it is immediately necessary to protect against another's unlawful force.
- **Stand Your Ground**: Texas law imposes no duty to retreat if the actor had a legal right to be present, did not provoke the encounter, and was not engaged in criminal activity (Tex. Penal Code § 9.31(e)).
- **Exceptions**: Self-defense is barred if the actor provoked the encounter (unless abandoning and communicating intent, § 9.31(b)(4)) or reacted to verbal provocation alone (§ 9.31(b)(1)).
- **Burden of Proof**: Once raised, the prosecution carries the burden of persuasion to disprove self-defense beyond a reasonable doubt.

### 22. Assault Public Servant in Texas? How To Beat Your Criminal Case (2025)

**Scenario:** During an altercation or medical emergency, an individual makes accidental contact with a first responder and faces third-degree felony charges.

**Legal Strategy:**
- **Statutory Enhancement**: Tex. Penal Code § 22.01(b)(1) enhances assault to a third-degree felony (2–10 years imprisonment) if committed against a known public servant discharging official duties; subjective pain satisfies bodily injury without visible wounds.
- **Primary Defenses**:
  1. *Lack of Knowledge*: The accused lacked reasonable awareness the individual was a public servant (e.g., plainclothes officer, unannounced entry).
  2. *Involuntary Act / Lack of Mens Rea*: Contact was accidental or caused by medical distress, not intentional, knowing, or reckless conduct (Tex. Penal Code § 6.01).
  3. *Excessive Force*: Self-defense against an officer is permitted under Tex. Penal Code § 9.31(c) if the officer used excessive force before resistance.

### 23. BURGLARY CHARGES? A FORMER DA EXPLAINS EVERYTHING (2021)

**Scenario:** An individual enters a building or habitation without authorization and faces felony burglary charges rather than misdemeanor trespass.

**Legal Strategy:**
- **Statutory Distinction**:
  - *Burglary (Tex. Penal Code § 30.02)*: Entering a habitation or building without effective consent with intent to commit a felony, theft, or assault; second-degree felony (2–20 years).
  - *Criminal Trespass (Tex. Penal Code § 30.05)*: Entering or remaining without consent after notice; Class B or A misdemeanor.
- **Intent Requirement**: The state must prove felonious or theft intent at the moment of entry; intoxication, mistake, seeking shelter, or retrieving property negates intent.
- **Pre-Indictment Intervention**: Present evidence of non-criminal intent to a grand jury under Tex. Code Crim. Proc. art. 20A to secure a "No Bill" or reduction to misdemeanor trespass.

### 24. Bail Bonds in Texas: Learn Your Options! Can A Lawyer Help?

**Scenario:** An arrestee held in county jail must evaluate bail options to secure pretrial release affordably.

**Legal Strategy:**
- **Bail Mechanisms**:
  1. *Cash Bond*: Full amount deposited with court; refundable upon case disposition less administrative fees.
  2. *Personal Recognizance (PR) Bond (Tex. Code Crim. Proc. art. 17.03)*: Release without surety upon sworn promise to appear, often conditioned on reporting/testing.
  3. *Surety Bond*: Commercial bondsman fee (10–15% non-refundable premium).
- **Attorney Bonds & Writs**: Attorneys acting as sureties can post bond directly against legal fees, avoiding separate bondsman expenses. Counsel can file a Motion for Bond Reduction or Application for Writ of Habeas Corpus under Tex. Code Crim. Proc. art. 17.15, challenging excessive bail under the Eighth Amendment and Texas Constitution art. I, § 13.

### 25. Break Free From Probation: The Secrets To Early Release! (2023)

**Scenario:** A compliant probationer seeks early termination of their Texas community supervision term.

**Legal Strategy:**
- **Statutory Authority**: Tex. Code Crim. Proc. art. 42A.701 governs early termination of community supervision:
  - *Straight Probation*: Eligible after completing one-third of the sentence or two years, whichever is less.
  - *Deferred Adjudication*: Discretionary early termination permitted at any point during supervision.
- **Statutory Exclusions**: Intoxication offenses (Tex. Penal Code Ch. 49), sex offenses, and Tex. Code Crim. Proc. art. 42A.540 ("3G" offenses) are statutorily ineligible for early release.
- **Requirements & Caution**: Complete all restitution, community service, and courses; a failed drug screen triggers a Motion to Revoke (MTR) or Adjudicate.

### 26. Can A Criminal Jury Ignore The Law? Jury Nullification Explained (2023)

**Scenario:** A defendant faces severe felony penalties for a low-level or technical statutory violation, raising whether a jury can refuse to convict.

**Legal Strategy:**
- **Jury Nullification Principle**: A trial jury possesses the unreviewable constitutional power to return a "Not Guilty" verdict contrary to the evidence if it finds the statute or prosecution unjust.
- **Judicial Prohibition**: Judges strictly forbid defense counsel from explicitly arguing for nullification or advising jurors to disregard statutory instructions.
- **Advocacy Approach**: Weave themes of disproportionate punishment and equity into closing arguments; under Texas law, a single holdout juror adhering to reasonable doubt forces a hung jury and mistrial.

### 27. Can An Open Container Lead To A DWI Arrest in Texas (2025)

**Scenario:** A driver is stopped for a traffic infraction and questioned after officers observe an open alcoholic container belonging to a passenger.

**Legal Strategy:**
- **Statutory Offense**: Tex. Penal Code § 49.031 makes possessing an open container in the passenger area on a public highway a Class C misdemeanor, cited against the driver regardless of passenger ownership.
- **Investigative Escalation**: An open container supplies reasonable suspicion to prolong the stop and order Standardized Field Sobriety Tests (SFSTs).
- **Practical Caution**: Transport alcohol in a locked trunk or glove box; roadside sobriety tests carry high false-positive arrest risks for sober drivers.

### 28. Can a Criminal Case Be Dismissed Based Upon A Victim's Request? (2022)

**Scenario:** A complainant in an assault or property case requests that prosecutors dismiss charges following an out-of-court reconciliation.

**Legal Strategy:**
- **Prosecutorial Authority**: Criminal charges are brought by the State of Texas; complainants are prosecuting witnesses without legal authority to dismiss cases.
- **Offense Variation**:
  - *Domestic Violence / Violent Felonies*: DA "No Drop" policies routinely proceed with prosecution regardless of victim objections.
  - *Property Offenses & Minor Misdemeanors*: Full restitution accompanied by a complainant's non-prosecution request frequently facilitates conditional dismissals.
- **Affidavit of Non-Prosecution (ANP)**: Execute a notarized ANP clarifying lack of intent or injury to provide defense counsel leverage during plea bargaining or grand jury review.

### 29. Carrying A Gun In Public? A Former DA Breaks Down The Law! (2021)

**Scenario:** A lawful gun owner carrying a firearm in their vehicle is arrested for weapons violations during a traffic stop.

**Legal Strategy:**
- **Unlawful Carrying of Weapons (UCW)**: Under Tex. Penal Code § 46.02, carrying a handgun in a motor vehicle is lawful under the traveling presumption unless the person is engaged in criminal activity (beyond Class C traffic offenses), prohibited from possession, or carrying in plain view without a holster.
- **Nexus to Other Offenses**: Allegations of DWI (Tex. Penal Code § 49.04) or drug possession transform lawful carriage into a Class A misdemeanor UCW charge.
- **Felon Firearms Prohibition**: Tex. Penal Code § 46.04 prohibits firearm possession by individuals convicted of a felony or family violence assault for five years following release from confinement or supervision; violation is a third-degree felony.

### 30. Case Dismissed - Does It Automatically Come Off My Criminal Record? (2025)

**Scenario:** Following dismissal of a criminal case, an individual discovers the arrest record continues to appear on background check databases.

**Legal Strategy:**
- **No Automatic Expungement**: Dismissals, acquittals, or grand jury no-bills do not erase criminal history; arrest data remains in public databases until an expunction is granted.
- **Statutory Expunction (Tex. Code Crim. Proc. Ch. 55A)**: Requires filing a verified civil petition in district court against all arresting and custodial agencies.
- **Waiting Periods**:
  - *Felonies (No-Billed)*: Must await statute of limitations expiration (typically 3 years) unless dismissed for lack of probable cause.
  - *Misdemeanors*: 180 days (Class C) to 1–2 years (Class A/B) depending on limitations.
  - *Pretrial Diversion*: Statutorily eligible for immediate expunction upon certified program completion.

### 31. Change This Email Setting to Keep Cops OUT of Your Inbox

**Scenario:** Law enforcement obtains historical email communications directly from a third-party email provider without a search warrant.

**Legal Strategy:**
- **Stored Communications Act (18 U.S.C. § 2703)**: Provisions treating emails stored on a third-party server exceeding 180 days as "abandoned" historically allowed government acquisition via subpoena rather than a warrant.
- **Constitutional Challenges**: *United States v. Warshak*, 631 F.3d 266 (6th Cir. 2010), held that users have a reasonable expectation of privacy in stored emails requiring a Fourth Amendment probable cause warrant.
- **Protective Protocols**: Use end-to-end encryption (PGP/Signal), download communications to local hardware, and utilize POP3 protocols that delete messages from remote servers upon receipt.

### 32. Charged With A Crime? Does Being A Good Person Help Your Criminal Case? (2022)

**Scenario:** A defendant with an unblemished personal background seeks to present character evidence to influence the outcome of their criminal case.

**Legal Strategy:**
- **Bifurcated Trial Rules (Tex. Code Crim. Proc. art. 37.07)**:
  - *Guilt/Innocence Phase*: Introducing good character evidence under Tex. R. Evid. 404(a)(2) "opens the door" to prosecutorial cross-examination with specific bad acts under Rule 404(b).
  - *Punishment Phase & Negotiations*: Character letters and achievements provide critical leverage during negotiations for pretrial diversion, deferred adjudication, or shock probation under Tex. Code Crim. Proc. art. 42A.202.

### 33. Charged With A Felony in Texas? Why You Must Get It Dismissed! (2026)

**Scenario:** An individual is indicted on felony charges carrying statutory penalties from state jail incarceration up to life imprisonment.

**Legal Strategy:**
- **Supervision Types**:
  - *Straight Probation*: Conviction entered; suspends sentence but establishes permanent felon status.
  - *Deferred Adjudication (Tex. Code Crim. Proc. art. 42A.101)*: Finding of guilt deferred; completion avoids conviction and allows petitioning for an Order of Nondisclosure under Tex. Gov't Code Ch. 411.
- **Collateral Consequences**: Felony convictions trigger lifetime federal firearm bans (18 U.S.C. § 922(g)(1)), disenfranchisement during sentence/parole, license loss, and mandatory deportation for non-citizens.
- **Grand Jury Strategy**: Submit an exculpatory packet under Tex. Code Crim. Proc. Ch. 20A seeking a "No Bill" to preserve eligibility for civil expunction.

### 34. Child Endangerment: A Former DA Breaks Down The Law! (2021)

**Scenario:** A parent briefly leaves a child unattended in a vehicle or residence and is arrested for child endangerment or abandonment.

**Legal Strategy:**
- **Statutory Offense**: Tex. Penal Code § 22.041 distinguishes *Abandoning a Child* (leaving a child under 15 without reasonable care) from *Endangering a Child* (intentionally, knowingly, recklessly, or with criminal negligence placing a child under 15 in imminent danger of death, bodily injury, or impairment); State Jail Felony (180 days to 2 years).
- **Reasonableness Standard**: The statute applies an objective "reasonable person" standard evaluated by vehicle temperature, duration, and proximity to hazards.
- **Defensive Strategies**: Present evidence to the grand jury demonstrating lack of imminent danger and affirmative safety precautions (climate control active, line of sight maintained) to secure a "No Bill" or dismissal.

### 35. Conditional Dismissals: Learn How To Get Your Criminal Case Dismissed (2025)

**Scenario:** A first-time offender facing strong state evidence seeks a negotiated resolution that prevents a final criminal conviction.

**Legal Strategy:**
- **Conditional Dismissal Mechanism**: Defense counsel negotiates an informal contract dismissal conditioned on upfront restitution, community service, or targeted classes (e.g., theft or drug education).
- **Advantage Over Deferred Adjudication**: Completing upfront conditions yields an outright dismissal without entering a guilty plea or serving supervised probation.
- **Expunction Preservation**: An outright dismissal preserves eligibility for complete civil expunction under Tex. Code Crim. Proc. Ch. 55A after statutory limitations expire or upon prosecutorial consent.

### 36. Continuous Family Violence in Texas: Learn the Law & Your Criminal Defenses (2025)

**Scenario:** An individual is accused of two domestic altercations within twelve months and faces felony continuous family violence charges.

**Legal Strategy:**
- **Statutory Enhancement**: Tex. Penal Code § 25.11 establishes Continuous Violence Against the Family as a third-degree felony (2–10 years prison) upon proof of two or more domestic assaults causing bodily injury under § 22.01(a)(1) within a 12-month period.
- **Non-Unanimous Jury Rule**: Jurors need not agree unanimously on which specific two assaults occurred—only that at least two occurred within the timeframe.

- Bodycam Evidence: Subpoena body-worn camera footage under Tex. Occ. Code § 1701.661 to establish that the arrest hinged on arbitrary chance rather than individualized probable cause.
- Legal Remedies: Move to suppress resulting evidence and statements under CCP Art. 38.23; documented lack of probable cause defeats qualified immunity in 42 U.S.C. § 1983 civil rights actions.

### Unauthorized use of a motor vehicle vs. auto theft-what's the difference?

**Scenario:** An individual operates a motor vehicle without the owner's express permission and faces criminal charges.

**Legal Strategy:**
- Statutory Distinctions:
  * Unauthorized Use of a Motor Vehicle (UUMV - TPC § 31.07): Operating another's vehicle without effective consent; State Jail Felony (180 days to 2 years).
  * Auto Theft (TPC § 31.03): Requires specific intent to permanently deprive the owner of the vehicle; graded by market value (typically 3rd or 2nd Degree Felony).
- UUMV Defenses:
  * Mistake of Fact & Consent (TPC § 8.02): Prior lending patterns, domestic relationships, or employer permission establish effective consent.
  * Lack of Knowledge: The state must prove the driver knew consent was lacking; driving a borrowed car unaware that it was unauthorized negates intent.

### mistakes gun owners make when carrying a firearm

**Scenario:** A gun owner carrying a firearm violates Texas carry restrictions or commits roadside protocol errors.

**Legal Strategy:**
- Statutory Carry Violations:
  * Carrying While Intoxicated (TPC § 46.02(a-6)): Unlawful to carry a handgun while intoxicated, even with an LTC.
  * Prohibited Locations (TPC § 46.03): Felony offense to carry in schools, polling places, courts, racetracks, or airport secured areas.
  * Firearm Trespass (TPC §§ 30.06, 30.07): Carrying past compliant 30.06 (concealed) or 30.07 (open) signs constitutes criminal trespass.
  * Child Access (TPC § 46.13): Leaving an unsecured loaded gun accessible to a child under 17 is a misdemeanor.
- Roadside Protocol: Keep hands on the wheel, avoid sudden movements, and notify the officer before retrieving documents.

### What if police show up at your front door and shove their foot in the doorway?

**Scenario:** An officer prevents a resident from closing their front door by wedging a foot across the threshold.

**Legal Strategy:**
- Threshold Protection (Payton v. New York): The Fourth Amendment strictly protects the home's threshold. Wedging a foot across the doorway is a warrantless physical entry, presumptively unlawful without consent or exigent circumstances.
- Verbal Protocol: State clearly: 'Officer, I do not consent to entry; please remove your foot from my doorway. Do you have a warrant?' Never push the officer or slam the door, which triggers Assault on a Peace Officer (TPC § 22.01(b)(1)) or Resisting Search (TPC § 38.03) charges.
- Suppression: Move under CCP Art. 38.23 to suppress all plain-view observations, contraband, and statements resulting from the illegal entry.

### A parent was arrested for letting their 8-year-old walk half a mile home

**Scenario:** A parent is investigated for child endangerment after letting an elementary-aged child walk home unsupervised.

**Legal Strategy:**
- Statutory Offense (TPC § 22.041): Child Abandonment or Endangerment requires intentionally, knowingly, recklessly, or negligently placing a child under 15 in imminent danger of injury or death; State Jail Felony. Imminent danger requires an immediate, non-speculative threat (Millslagle v. State).
- Parental Discretion: Texas Family Code protects reasonable parental judgment permitting independent childhood activities without constituting neglect.
- Defense Action: Document neighborhood safety, daytime visibility, and child maturity. Counsel presents an exculpatory packet to prosecutors and CPS/DFPS to secure dismissal and an administrative 'Rule-Out.'

### Police peering through your windows-what's legal?

**Scenario:** Police officers enter private property and peer through residential windows without a warrant.

**Legal Strategy:**
- Curtilage Protection (Florida v. Jardines; United States v. Dunn): Curtilage shares the Fourth Amendment protections of the home. While officers may use normal walkways to knock on the front door, veering off paths to peer into side or rear windows is an unlawful trespassory search.
- Sensory Devices (Kyllo v. United States): Using specialized thermal or optical devices not in general public use to inspect home interiors requires a warrant.
- Suppression Remedy: Move to suppress under CCP Art. 38.23; search warrants obtained using trespassory curtilage observations are invalid under the fruit of the poisonous tree doctrine.

### Cops claim "inventory search" to search your car without a warrant

**Scenario:** Police impound a stopped vehicle and search closed containers under the guise of an administrative inventory.

**Legal Strategy:**
- Inventory Criteria (South Dakota v. Opperman; Colorado v. Bertine): A warrantless inventory search is lawful only if: (1) impoundment is legally authorized; (2) conducted under standardized, written department policies; and (3) performed to catalog property, not as an investigative pretext.
- Reasonable Alternatives: Under Texas law, impoundment is improper if viable alternatives exist (legally parked, licensed passenger, or private tow).
- Closed Containers (Florida v. Wells): Opening closed containers during inventory is unconstitutional without explicit written departmental policy.
- Suppression: Subpoena police department impound policies and move to suppress evidence under CCP Art. 38.23.

### You get pulled over for a minor traffic violation and cops try an "inventory search"

**Scenario:** Police initiate vehicle impoundment and an inventory search following a minor roadside traffic stop.

**Legal Strategy:**
- Pretextual Impoundment: Impoundment cannot be automatic; Texas law requires it to be genuinely necessary to protect public safety or property. Offering viable alternatives (leaving the car legally parked or calling a licensed driver) negates the legal necessity of impounding.
- Verbal Refusal: State on camera: 'The vehicle is safe; I do not consent to impoundment or any search of my car.'
- Suppression Motion: Subpoena towing policies, CAD logs, and inventory slips under CCP Art. 39.14; move to suppress under CCP Art. 38.23 because rejecting reasonable alternatives rendered the impoundment pretextual.

### You get pulled over for a minor traffic violation-cops try to impound and "inventory" search your car

**Scenario:** Police impound a vehicle after a minor traffic infraction and discover contraband inside closed containers.

**Legal Strategy:**
- Core Standards (South Dakota v. Opperman; Florida v. Wells): Inventory searches are strictly administrative caretaking procedures to safeguard property; they are unconstitutional if used as an investigatory pretext.
- Container Scrutiny: Searching closed or locked containers requires standardized departmental directives; unguided searches through bags or compartments violate the Fourth Amendment.
- Pretext & Suppression: Show that the car was safely parked or eligible for release to a passenger. Impeach incomplete inventory logs under CCP Art. 39.14 to demonstrate an investigatory motive, requiring suppression under CCP Art. 38.23.
