# Newspaper

Date: 2026-05-31  
Category: english-writing

## Summary

This study note organizes newspaper English from class into article summaries, vocabulary groups, sentence patterns, phrasal verbs, review lists, practice prompts, and a mini quiz. The goal is to make news vocabulary easier to understand, remember, and use in speaking or writing.

## Key Points

- Newspaper English often uses compact phrases to describe changes, problems, causes, and consequences.
- Business and economy articles commonly describe hiring, layoffs, prices, competition, revenue, and market pressure.
- Local news articles often describe traffic, repairs, closures, public safety, and official updates.
- Many useful expressions are phrase-based, so it is better to practice full phrases instead of isolated words.

## How to Review

1. Read one article summary first.
2. Study the vocabulary table for that topic.
3. Practice the reusable sentence patterns aloud.
4. Cover the advanced vocabulary and try to recall it from the simple phrase.
5. Finish with the practice prompts and mini quiz.

## Article Summaries

### Article 1: Labor Market And Hiring

The article explains that the job market has changed after a period of very active hiring. Unemployment may still be low, but rapid wage growth and frenzied turnover are starting to abate. Some companies are becoming more careful, slowing hiring, or freezing hiring altogether.

Main idea: the labor market is still active, but it may be losing some of its earlier strength.

### Article 2: Competition And Rising Prices

The article describes a market where demand, competition, and costs are pushing prices higher. Companies may try to sweeten the deal for customers, but steep competition and rising costs can make it harder to keep prices low.

Main idea: strong demand and steep competition can drive up prices and change how companies compete.

### Article 3: Company Layoffs And Financial Pressure

The article discusses how a once high-flying company can face a reversal of fortune. If revenue slows or costs rise, the company may slash expenses, reduce its workforce, and come under public scrutiny.

Main idea: even successful companies can struggle when growth slows and financial pressure increases.

### Article 4: Local Road Closure And Bridge Repair

The article reports that a road or bridge repair project may cause lane closures and traffic delays. Officials may explain that repairs will take place over the weekend and that the schedule is subject to change.

Main idea: local news uses formal, precise language to explain public works, closures, directions, and safety updates.

## Reusable Sentence Patterns

| Pattern | Use it for | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `A is grappling with B.` | A person, company, city, or country is trying to deal with a difficult problem. | The company is grappling with slower revenue growth. |
| `A drove up B.` | A cause made prices, demand, or numbers increase. | Strong demand drove up housing prices. |
| `A may be losing its sizzle.` | Something may be becoming less exciting or powerful. | The job market may be losing its sizzle. |
| `The tables have turned.` | The situation has reversed. | Last year workers had more power, but now the tables have turned. |
| `The schedule is subject to change.` | A plan may change later. | The road closure schedule is subject to change. |
| `A marked a reversal of fortune.` | An event showed a major change from success to trouble, or the opposite. | The layoffs marked a reversal of fortune. |
| `Officials said the work will take place on DATE.` | Formal local news reporting. | Officials said the repairs will take place this weekend. |

## Phrasal Verbs And Fixed Expressions

| Phrase | Meaning / usage | Natural example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| grapple with | To try hard to deal with a difficult problem. | The city is grappling with traffic delays. |
| play out | To develop or end in a particular way. | I wonder how this situation will play out. |
| drive up | To cause something to increase. | Limited supply drove up prices. |
| die down | To become weaker, quieter, or less active. | The excitement died down after a few weeks. |
| sweeten the deal | To make an offer more attractive. | The seller lowered the price to sweeten the deal. |
| take place | To happen. | The repairs will take place overnight. |
| down the drain | Wasted or lost. | All that effort went down the drain. |
